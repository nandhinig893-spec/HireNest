ATS Resume Mistakes That Get You Rejected Before a Human Sees It

14 August 2026 · 4 min read

Most students and fresh graduates lose the job before a recruiter even opens their resume. The reason is usually an Applicant Tracking System, or ATS — software that scans and ranks resumes automatically before a human ever sees them. If the format trips the system, a strong candidate gets filtered out silently.

Here are the mistakes we see most often, based on reviewing resumes against real ATS parsing behaviour.

1. Tables and multi-column layouts

Visually appealing two-column resumes look great to a human eye, but many ATS parsers read left to right in a straight line — scrambling your work experience with your skills list into unreadable order. Stick to a single-column layout.

2. Text inside images or graphics

Icons for your phone number, graphic skill bars, or a photo-based header may not be readable by the system at all. If it's an image, assume the ATS can't see it.

3. Creative section headings

"My Journey" instead of "Experience," or "Toolbox" instead of "Skills," might feel more personal — but ATS software is trained to look for standard section names. Unusual headings can cause entire sections to be skipped.

4. Missing keywords from the job description

ATS software ranks resumes partly by keyword match. If the job posting says "cross-functional collaboration" and your resume says "worked well with others," you may rank lower even if you mean the same thing. Mirror the language of the posting where it's honestly true of your experience.

5. Saving as the wrong file type

Some systems still struggle with certain PDF exports, especially ones created from design tools rather than Word. A clean .docx is often the safest format unless the application explicitly asks for PDF.

Quick self-check: Copy-paste your resume into a plain text file. If the sentences run together, sections merge, or the order looks scrambled — that's roughly what an ATS may be seeing.

The fix

You don't need to overhaul your resume from scratch — you need a format that was built with ATS parsing in mind from the start, so you can focus on the content instead of second-guessing the layout.
